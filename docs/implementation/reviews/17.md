## Findings and fixes

1. **P1 — a purchase could top up another customer or invent its price.** The
   submitted service accepted unrelated entitlements, orders, recipients and
   sellers, and trusted a quote whose amount/currency did not match the order
   item. It now binds the purchase to the original payer, seller and holder,
   rejects reuse of the original plan item, and requires the immutable price
   facts to match.

2. **P1 — money could be reserved or settled through unrelated accounts.** A
   caller could reserve another payer's service credit, settle another
   purchase's reservation, or supply an arbitrary revenue account. Top-ups now
   bind one unique reservation; reserve/settle validate event, amount,
   currency, payer ownership, account kind and system-owned revenue.

3. **P1 — paid credit could be lost after supplier failure.** `fail_top_up` and
   the cap-raise failure path could move paid/provisioning/unknown purchases to
   `FAILED` without refunding money or granting allowance. Only requested or
   held purchases may now fail directly. Paid credit returns to `PAID` for an
   explicit retry/refund, and lost cap responses have an observation-only
   reconciliation path that never blindly resends.

4. **P1 — documented top-up history was mutable and its states were weakly
   constrained.** PostgreSQL now rejects purchase-fact rewrites/deletes,
   restricts transitions to the recoverable state graph, permits reservation
   binding and application time only on their corresponding transitions, and
   requires a reservation for every reserved-or-later state.

5. **P1 — read-then-insert and stale-row races broke idempotency.** Transaction
   advisory locks now serialize control creation, top-up identity, budget
   checks and notice identity. Top-up mutations also reload the locked row.
   Concurrent request replay converges on one row, while ledger row locking
   continues to arbitrate competing credit reservations.

6. **P1 — an approved bounded-exposure row could promise an unquantified or
   inactive guarantee.** Service and database constraints now require an
   active state, named policy reference, non-negative quantified maximum and a
   confirmed active hard limit before either enforcement claim can be read as
   a promise.

7. **P1 — supplier/profile capability was inferred too loosely.** A missing or
   different synchronous limit response is no longer accepted as the requested
   cap. Applying a top-up also requires the adapter to advertise verified
   `TOPUP` support. Current Telnyx configuration advertises neither top-up nor
   spending enforcement, so a documented data-limit PATCH cannot silently
   become evidence for line reuse or a hard prepaid promise.

8. **P1 — cross-resource operations lacked ownership checks.** A top-up could
   be applied to another line, an entitlement could suspend/resume another
   customer's line, and a carrier adapter could act on a line belonging to a
   different supplier. Each operation now validates the entitlement/line and
   line/adapter relationships before changing state or contacting a supplier.

9. **P2 — model metadata creation failed outside Alembic.** The trigger's
   PostgreSQL exception placeholders were not escaped for SQLAlchemy `DDL`, so
   schema-isolated tests failed during `SQLModel.metadata.create_all`. The
   placeholders are escaped in model metadata while remaining native in the
   migration, and the schema-isolation suite now passes.

10. **P2 — notice configuration accepted invalid and duplicate thresholds.**
    Thresholds are now constrained to 1–100 and normalized once, preventing
    impossible percentages and duplicate work before the database uniqueness
    backstop.

11. **P2 — a legacy cascade test was not repeatable on a shared database.** It
    asserted that the entire packages table was empty after deleting one user,
    so an unrelated row left by any earlier run caused a false failure. The
    assertion now scopes to the deleted user's id and passes repeatedly against
    a deliberately contaminated review database.

## Remaining gates

- **D1 remains open.** No real Telnyx top-up or supplier-enforced limit has
  been observed. Telnyx's current documented/fixture capability record
  explicitly withholds `TOPUP` and `SPENDING_ENFORCEMENT`; live reuse, closed-app
  exhaustion and cap latency/overshoot still require supplier and device
  evidence.
- **D5 remains open.** No bounded-exposure approval exists, and the commercial
  policy for tariff charging within an allowance remains undecided. The
  mechanism is accepted; no policy row is seeded and no promise is enabled.
- Shared carrier/internet credit remains refused until carrier exposure is
  independently bounded while the app is closed.
- Notices are recorded but not delivered, and no scheduler drives exhaustion,
  expiry or reconciliation. Those runtime integrations remain later chunks.
- Organization budgets remain the deliberately minimal per-currency precursor
  to chunk 24's periods, reporting and offboarding policy.
