## Findings and fixes

1. **P1 — the submitted stack was based on unaccepted predecessors.** Chunks
   09–14 were rebuilt sequentially on accepted `develop`, preserving the
   accepted chunk 07/08 history instead of reverting it.

2. **P1 — payment and refund postings did not form a coherent reversal.** A
   successful routed capture now posts `settlement_clearing → revenue` in the
   same transaction as the paid state. A settled external refund posts the
   exact `revenue → settlement_clearing` reversal; it no longer debits an
   unrelated customer service-credit account.

3. **P1 — refund replay and outcome transitions were not semantic or final.**
   Reusing a business key for a different attempt, amount, reason or policy now
   fails. Outcome handlers lock current rows, require a processor reference for
   success, resolve unknown exceptions, and reject late success/failure changes
   after a terminal outcome. Requested, pending and unknown refunds continue to
   consume headroom.

4. **P1 — persisted refund and dispute facts could disagree with their charge.**
   Composite foreign keys bind attempt id, processor and currency. Terminal
   refunds and disputes are immutable in PostgreSQL.

5. **P1 — dispute notifications were unbounded and resolved disputes could be
   rewritten.** Only captured attempts are disputable; aggregate non-won
   disputes cannot exceed the charge. Duplicate references are validated
   semantically, resolution is locked/idempotent, and the exception item is
   closed with the outcome.

6. **P1 — bank evidence could race, lose actor/timestamp attribution, or be
   reclassified after credit.** Imports and exception identities use advisory
   locks and reject conflicting replay. Matching locks and refreshes the row,
   requires a customer service-credit account and named actor, binds account
   currency structurally, and freezes matched evidence. The database now
   requires account, actor and timestamp together.

7. **P1 — snapshots were copied but issued documents remained editable.**
   Snapshot input is deep-copied and issued financial documents are immutable
   at the database boundary. Negative totals and empty document numbers fail.

8. **P1 — the submitted settlement helper did not ingest a processor report or
   reconcile the ledger.** Immutable processor settlement records now preserve
   gross, refunds, disputes, fees, tax, charge/settlement currencies, FX and net
   payout. Reconciliation scopes local records by processor/currency/period,
   verifies both clearing and revenue/adjustment postings, checks payout
   arithmetic, and raises a durable exception for every mismatch. A batch seam
   is available to the eventual scheduler.

9. **P2 — clean metadata creation failed in the integrated stack.** Chunk 11's
   outbox lease token used an untyped SQLAlchemy column. It is now explicitly a
   UUID, and a from-empty-database `SQLModel.metadata.create_all()` probe passes.

The submitted documentation also overstated the ledger balance trigger: a
balanced journal entry does not enforce a cross-row refund ceiling. The review
record and data-model amendment now state the actual lock and ledger guarantees.
