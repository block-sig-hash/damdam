## Findings and fixes

1. **P1 — product allowance could change after purchase.** Fulfilment reads the
   one allowance row referenced by an order's product, but that row allowed
   update and delete. A delayed order could therefore receive a different data,
   voice or validity grant from the one sold. Product allowances are now
   immutable in PostgreSQL; changing an offer requires a new product identity.

2. **P1 — one supplier line could satisfy two order items.** Idempotent adoption
   keyed only on provider/reference and returned an existing line without
   checking its entitlement. A repeated or collided provider reference could
   mark another item fulfilled without giving it service. Adoption now verifies
   the existing entitlement belongs to the same order item.

3. **P1 — lifecycle and credential operations accepted crossed resources.** A
   line action could be dispatched or settled against another line, and an
   activation credential fetched from one line could be stored under another
   installation. Line/action, line/adapter and line/installation identities are
   now checked before the external call or state change.

4. **P1 — a credential grant could be issued to an unrelated user.** The grant
   token was bound to the requested subject but issuance never proved that
   subject held the entitlement. Issuance now checks the credential's
   installation and entitlement, and redemption repeats the check so a grant
   stops working after reassignment.

5. **P2 — the submitted stack predated accepted dependency fixes.** Chunk 15
   was replayed onto the accepted chunk-14 integration before review. The only
   conflict was the status record; accepted reviews 09–14 were preserved.

Regression coverage demonstrates each defect against PostgreSQL before and
after the fix.
