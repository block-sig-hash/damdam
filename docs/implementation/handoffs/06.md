## Founder decisions recorded during this chunk

The assignment says to implement email recovery *"if adopted in chunk 01"*. It
was not: `DECISIONS.md` carried it as a **proposed default** and the only
`#### Decision —` entry was the empty template. I stopped and asked rather than
assume, and two decisions were given and are now recorded with dates:

1. **Email is the launch account identity and primary recovery channel.** Phone
   numbers remain *optional* verified identifiers for service-specific uses —
   calling, provisioning, notifications. **Recovery must not require a SIM.**
2. **The pre-verification enumeration signal is removed**, with equivalent
   resistance on the email endpoints, and abuse controls preserved so the
   uniform response cannot be used for SMS or email flooding.

## What changed

### Identity core

`account_identifiers` and `identity_tokens`, a delivery-transport abstraction,
and `IdentityService`. Three rules, all enforced by the database rather than by
control flow:

- **Single-use** via `UPDATE … WHERE consumed_at IS NULL`, so two simultaneous
  clicks cannot both succeed; **expiry exclusive** at the boundary; **purpose
  bound into the row**. A wrong purpose reports `identity_token_invalid`, the
  same as an unknown token — telling a holder they have "the wrong kind" would
  tell them what they have.
- **An unverified claim grants nothing and blocks nobody.** Uniqueness is a
  *partial* index over verified rows, so claiming someone's address can never be
  confirmed once they own it, and equally cannot deny them from verifying it.
- **Only token hashes are stored.**

### Email endpoints

`/v1/auth/email/verify/{request,confirm}` and
`/v1/auth/email/recovery/{request,confirm}`. Recovery issues a session from an
emailed token with no OTP and no phone, revokes every existing session, and
invalidates other outstanding recovery tokens so an older link cannot be
replayed after.

### Enumeration

`POST /auth/otp/request` returned `account_exists` / `account_not_found`
unauthenticated — a working oracle for whether **any** phone number had an
account. Removed, along with both status mappings and their EN/FR strings.
AC-01.7's "direct to login" survives as `is_new_user` on verify, reachable only
by someone who received and submitted the code.

### Legacy accounts

Migration `0028_account_identity` backfills each existing account's OTP-proved
phone as a verified primary identifier — idempotent, verified by seeding users at
`0027` and upgrading. Nobody is stranded and nobody is asked to re-prove a
number they already log in with. `users.phone_number` is untouched.

## Two corrections worth reading

Both were caught by failing tests, and both were me over-correcting:

1. **I unified the OTP rate-limit budget across signup and recovery**, reasoning
   that separate budgets might leak existence through a side channel. Wrong
   twice: the scope follows the endpoint the caller chose, not account state, so
   it leaks nothing — and sharing a budget broke a real flow, where someone signs
   up, gets PIN-locked and recovers immediately. Reverted; `purpose` is now
   derived separately from the rate scope so a recovery code still cannot be
   redeemed as a login code.
2. **The same mistake in the identity throttle**, where linking an address then
   recovering with it tripped the limit. Also scoped per flow now.

## Known limitations and open questions

1. **Email is now the account identity, but there is no email *login* flow.**
   Recovery doubles as SIM-free access, which satisfies the decision as stated.
   Whether a first-class email login belongs in 06B or chunk 18 is a scoping
   question for the reviewer.
2. **Token TTL is one hour**, a default I chose. Not derived from a spec.
3. **`NullDeliveryTransport` silently discards.** Deliberate — a half-configured
   provider emailing real people from staging is worse — but it does mean a
   misconfigured deployment fails quietly. Worth a startup warning in 06B.
4. **Pre-existing, not mine:** `test_hto_model_postgres.py` and
   `test_auth_model_postgres.py` fail on a **second** run against the same
   database — they commit fixed values without cleanup. Confirmed by running them
   alone with none of my tests present. Same "fresh database" requirement chunk
   04 recorded. My own tests truncate and are repeatable. I did not modify those
   files, since changing accepted tests would invalidate their review records for
   an unrelated defect.
