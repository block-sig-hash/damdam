## The headline: the prepaid guarantee cannot be promised, and that is the answer

The assignment says *"use documented provider hard limits or a separately
approved bounded-exposure product policy; do not claim delayed app accounting
stops native calls."* `prd.md` AC-36.4 says the same thing. So the chunk's most
important output is a refusal:

`ControlService.prepaid_guarantee` returns **false** for Telnyx as configured
today, with the reason attached. Three ways to get a true, and delayed app-side
accounting is not one of them:

1. The supplier advertises verified `SPENDING_ENFORCEMENT`, the line's control is
   confirmed active, **and** the supplier documents a maximum overshoot.
2. Somebody approved a bounded-exposure policy with a named reference — accepting
   a quantified maximum rather than claiming zero.
3. Nothing else.

Telnyx fails at step 1 twice over: the capability is withheld because the
enforcement latency is undocumented, and even with it switched on, an
unquantified overshoot is not a cap. There is a test for each
(`test_a_hard_cap_is_not_promised_on_delayed_accounting`,
`test_an_enforceable_limit_with_unquantified_latency_still_cannot_promise`).

The calling amendment asks the same question about shared credit: *"Carrier
spend has its own reservation/control mechanism while the app is closed. A
unified displayed balance is insufficient."* `shared_credit_allowed` therefore
delegates to the same assessment and gives the same answer — one function is the
product promise and the other is the product shape, and they must not disagree.

**What this means commercially:** an offer promising "you cannot overspend" stays
gated. The unblocking inputs are D1 question 7 (what is `data_limit`'s
enforcement latency and maximum overshoot) or a D5 bounded-exposure approval.
Both are named in the gaps below.

## The six things worth reviewing closely

### 1. A top-up grants nothing until it is applied

Seven states, because a partial state is only recoverable if it has a name.
**Only `APPLIED` counts toward a balance.** A customer who has paid but whose
supplier cap has not been raised does not have the data, and telling them they do
produces a session that fails while the app insists it should work.

`uq_entitlement_top_ups_order_item` is the idempotency guarantee: one purchase,
one top-up, however many replays. `request_top_up` returns the existing row
rather than raising, so a worker retry is harmless rather than an incident.

Where a supplier cap is in force, `apply_top_up` **raises it before** granting
the allowance. A lost response there leaves `OUTCOME_UNKNOWN` and moves neither
the allowance nor the money: granting would give away data the carrier still
blocks, and refunding would return money for a cap that may well have been
raised.

### 2. A top-up never gives away allowance and never loses paid credit

Both halves of the assignment's last requirement, both tested.

`test_a_top_up_after_an_overshoot_does_not_give_away_the_overshoot`: a customer
who overshot by 200 MB and buys 500 MB has 300 MB. Usage already recorded stays
recorded; the top-up adds to a deficit rather than resetting it.

`test_a_failed_top_up_returns_the_hold_and_keeps_no_credit`: failing releases the
**reservation**, which posts nothing because nothing was ever spent. Money
already settled is a refund and is chunk 14's — `fail_top_up` refuses an applied
top-up outright rather than quietly reversing a grant.

### 3. Concurrency is arbitrated by the database, not by ordering

`test_two_concurrent_top_ups_cannot_both_spend_the_same_credit` runs two real
sessions through a barrier against a 2,000 balance with two 1,500 top-ups, and
asserts exactly one wins. The lock is chunk 10's `SELECT … FOR UPDATE`; this
chunk adds no second mechanism.

`test_a_suspension_and_a_resume_cannot_both_be_open` reaches chunk 15's partial
unique index through chunk 17's policy: a suspend racing a resume has no defined
answer, and the database refuses it before the carrier does.

### 4. Suspension is a request; two refusals matter more than the happy path

The line moves when the carrier confirms, not when we ask — chunk 15's four
states, driven by this chunk's policy.

- **An unobserved balance suspends nobody.** Cutting a customer off because a
  poller has never run is worse than the overshoot it would prevent, and the
  balance would be an invention anyway.
- **A carrier-imposed restriction is not ours to resume.** A line in
  `data_limit_exceeded` was not suspended by us. Only raising the limit clears
  it, so `resume_after_top_up` raises `carrier_imposed_restriction` and gives an
  operator something to act on instead of a supplier rejection nobody can read.

Late usage arriving *after* a suspension is still counted
(`test_late_usage_after_a_suspension_is_still_counted`). Discarding it would give
away the overshoot that caused the suspension.

### 5. `set_data_limit` is the one Telnyx call that is not asynchronous

`PATCH /sim_cards/{id}` returns `200` with the updated SIM card, not a `202` and
an action. So it returns a line rather than a `ProviderAction` — pretending
otherwise would invent a settlement step and leave a caller polling an action id
that never existed.

Two refusals in the adapter: a limit that is not a whole number of MB (the
documented unit) is rejected rather than rounded, because silently adjusting a
customer's cap is only noticed after a month; and a supplier that accepts the
call but then reports **no** limit is recorded as `FAILED`, not `ACTIVE` —
recording it as active would put a cap in our database that does not exist at the
carrier, which is the worst kind of wrong.

### 6. Notices announce once, and only about something measured

`uq_allowance_notices` is the mechanism. Usage corrected downward and back up
re-crosses 80%, and without the constraint the customer is notified again every
five minutes until they learn to turn notifications off — including the one that
matters.

An unobserved balance announces nothing, for the same reason it suspends nobody.

- The **bounded-exposure approval**. `approve_bounded_exposure` implements the

  charge.** Chunk 16 records both numbers and this chunk decides neither.

**The calling amendment's shared-credit precondition is not met**, and
`shared_credit_allowed` says so. A common carrier/internet spend pool is refused
until carrier exposure is independently bounded.

## Known gaps and handoff

- **Exhaustion while DamDam is closed is simulated, not observed.** The
  assignment asks to *"simulate and, where authorized, observe"* it; only the
  first half is possible without an account and a handset. The simulation is
  `test_late_usage_after_a_suspension_is_still_counted` and the exhaustion path
  around it. Observation is chunk 29's physical pilot.
- **No worker or scheduler.** `suspend_for_exhaustion`, `low_balance_notices` and
  the expiry check are driven by a caller. Wiring them to chunk 11's outbox is
  chunk 25/26.
- **Notices are recorded, not delivered.** `AllowanceNotice` records that a
  threshold was crossed; sending it through `app/notifications` is chunk 21's
  scope, and the localisation belongs there too.
- **Organization budgets are minimal on purpose.** A per-currency cap and a
  headroom check. Periods are the caller's `since` argument rather than a
  configured billing cycle; chunk 24 owns real budgets, reports and offboarding.
- **`TopUpQuote` is supplied by the caller.** Pricing a top-up is chunk 09's
  catalog and a quote; this chunk takes the numbers rather than deciding them, so
  nothing here can invent a price.
- **For Codex:** the judgement call most worth a second opinion is
  `apply_top_up` raising the supplier cap *inside* the apply step. The
  alternative is a separate, independently retryable provisioning step, which is
  cleaner but adds a state a customer can sit in with money spent and no
  allowance. I chose the shorter window; the `OUTCOME_UNKNOWN` state exists
  either way.
- No merge, deployment, vendor message, live purchase or live limit change has
  been performed.
