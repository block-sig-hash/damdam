## Result

### Previously

V02 holds money and never spends it, on purpose: *a reservation stays held until
every known supplier liability is final*. Nothing decided that it was final.
There was no record of what a call cost, no way to tell what it cost **us**, and
no durable clock — a call whose outcome was lost held its customer's money until
somebody noticed by hand.

### Now

A finished call is metered from the provider's own account of it, charged once
against the hold that funded it, and the remainder released — or, when liability
cannot be established, left held with a row in front of a human.

**New modules:**

- `app/calling/metering.py` — two pure functions. `talk_interval` reads the
  billable duration out of the legs; `charge_for` prices it at the rate the
  attempt froze at authorization.
- `app/calling/charging.py` — `CallChargingService`: settlement, corrections,
  supplier-cost recording, durable deadlines and mid-call enforcement.
- `app/worker.py` — `app.calling.resolve_due_deadlines`, on a 60-second beat.

**Schema — migration `0039_call_settlement`, three new tables:** `call_charges`
(one live row per attempt), `call_supplier_costs` (observations, never derived)
and `call_deadlines` (the clock that survives a restart). `exception_kind` gains
five values, into chunk 14's queue rather than a second one. Documented as
[data-model §6.55](../../data-model.md).

**API:** `GET /v1/calls/{id}` and `GET /v1/calls` gain a `charge` object —
absent while liability is open, `is_final: false` while the supplier could still
correct it. No new endpoint; `docs/api-spec.yaml` is **+48 / −0**.

**The decisions a reviewer should look at:**

1. **The destination leg is the only billable clock.** The amendment names
   summing leg durations as a defect, and client elapsed time is never consulted
   at all — a browser that closed and an app that was killed produce the same
   number here. `talk_interval` takes legs and returns one of four outcomes;
   there is no argument it could take from a handset.
2. **"We do not know" is not "it was free".** An answered leg with no recorded
   end returns `DEFERRED`: the hold is **not** released, a deadline is written
   and an exception is raised. Releasing there would un-fund a call that may
   still be connected — and `MeteringOutcome.INCOMPLETE` carries `seconds=None`
   rather than `0` precisely so no caller can treat the two as the same.
3. **The authorized maximum binds us, not only the customer.** If metered cost
   exceeds the hold, the hold is what settles and the difference is raised as
   `call_settlement_shortfall` — our exposure. The stored breakdown is reduced
   to match what actually posted, because `ck_call_charges_total_is_its_parts`
   requires a receipt to add up to itself.
4. **A correction moves the difference, never the whole amount.** Chunk 16's
   rule applied to calls: reversing and re-posting would put two transactions in
   the books where one adjustment happened, and a customer would see a refund
   and a charge for a call that was mispriced by four seconds.
5. **No hold is ever extended.** The hold is `max_charge_amount`, derived from
   `max_seconds`, which the provider also enforces as `time_limit_secs` — the
   authorized maximum is the whole liability, so there is nothing to extend.
   `enforce()` re-asks whether the call may continue and stops it when the
   answer changes. This is a deliberate deviation from the assignment's "renew
   reservations only when funds and budgets permit": renewing past the
   authorized maximum would charge a customer for a call they never agreed the
   price of. **Flagged for the reviewer as the one place the letter of the
   assignment and its intent point different ways.**
6. **Supplier cost is a separate number in both directions.** No retail amount
   is derived from a supplier cost and no supplier cost from a retail amount.
   V01 could not establish how many components a call bills, so the table counts
   observations and a person reconciles them.
7. **Deadlines are rows, not timers**, claimed with `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED`.
   The worker handles each in its own transaction: one attempt that cannot be
   resolved must not roll back the ten that could, or the queue never drains
   past its first bad row.
8. **Settlement is wired into the lifecycle, not bolted beside it.** When the
   last leg ends and the attempt moves to a terminal state, `_settle` runs in the
   same transaction as the event that caused it. `CallLifecycleService.charging`
   is optional, so a deployment without settlement configured **holds** its
   reservations rather than releasing them through a half-configured path.

**Deliberately deferred:** the mobile and browser clients (V04/V05), supplier
invoice reconciliation and FX, and margin reporting.

## Migrations and compatibility

- **Fresh install:** `alembic upgrade head` creates three tables and five enum
  labels. Every endpoint answers; `charge` is simply absent until a call settles.
- **Old-data upgrade:** additive only. No column is added to an existing table
  and no historical row is read, including the legacy voice tables chunk 04
  retained. A database carrying V02's schema upgrades with nothing to backfill.
- **Old-client compatibility:** `charge` is a new optional field on two existing
  responses. A client built against V02 ignores it. No request shape changes and
  no endpoint is removed.
- **Rollback:** `alembic downgrade 0038_call_authorization` drops exactly what
  the upgrade created. The five `exception_kind` labels stay — PostgreSQL cannot
  remove a label without rewriting the type, and a queue row that named one
  would lose the only word describing why a human was called. **Development
  rollback only:** dropping `call_charges` discards settlement records while the
  journal entries they posted remain, leaving the books without the rows that
  name what they were for.
- **Configuration:** one new setting, `CALLING_SUPPLIER_COST_WAIT_SECONDS`
  (86400). No new secret names.
- **Dependencies:** none added. Nothing new reaches the `aarch64` instance.

## Known gaps and handoff

- **B3 — no hard prepaid guarantee.** `time_limit_secs` bounds the destination
  leg and is the only control that survives this process dying; the parked client
  leg has no documented duration bound. Settlement therefore caps what a customer
  is charged but **cannot promise** the supplier stopped billing when we asked.
  The shortfall exception is the honest record of that gap, not a fix for it.
- **Organization call budgets are wired but inert in this branch.**
  `organization_call_headroom` composes chunk 17's `budget_headroom` with call
  spend and open call reservations, and is tested — but `main.py` passes no
  `ControlService`, because constructing one needs `UsageService` and
  `ConnectivityService` whose application wiring belongs to **chunk 20**, which
  is not merged. Building a second copy here would conflict on merge and give the
  deployment two control services disagreeing about one line. With no control
  service the method answers `None` — *nobody has recorded a decision* — rather
  than an unlimited budget. **This is the first thing to connect once chunk 20
  lands.**
- **Carrier and internet spend are not pooled.** `enforce()` returns the reason
  in words: without independently bounded carrier exposure there is no
  unrestricted common pool, per the amendment. Chunk 17's
  `shared_credit_allowed` already answers the same question and gives the same
  answer; this chunk does not override it.
- **Nothing ingests a CDR yet.** `record_supplier_cost` is complete and tested
  and has no production caller, because no live route can produce one. The
  reviewer should read it as a prepared seam, not as dead code — and should
  decide whether it belongs to V03 or to the chunk that turns the route on.
- **A provisional charge becomes final by elapsed time**, not by evidence, if no
  supplier record arrives within `CALLING_SUPPLIER_COST_WAIT_SECONDS`. That is a
  judgement call worth challenging: the alternative is books that never close.
- **For Codex:** the deviation in decision 5 (no hold extension) is the one to
  argue with first. Second is `settle()` returning a status rather than raising:
  three of its four outcomes are ordinary, and a caller that must catch
  exceptions for ordinary outcomes eventually catches them all in one place —
  but it does mean a caller can ignore a `DEFERRED` result.
- **No merge, deployment, store submission or production deletion was
  performed**, and none is authorized by this assignment.
