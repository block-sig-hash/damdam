## The four things worth reviewing closely

### 1. The refund ceiling counts money that has not come back yet

`refunded_total` includes refunds in `requested`, `pending` **and `unknown`**,
not only `succeeded`. A refund whose outcome we have not seen may already have
paid out. Excluding it is exactly how a second refund gets authorized on top of
a first, and the customer keeps both.

`request_refund` takes `SELECT … FOR UPDATE` on the payment attempt *before*
computing headroom, so two concurrent partial refunds cannot both see the same
room. `test_two_concurrent_partial_refunds_cannot_both_win` runs two real
sessions and asserts exactly one succeeds.

A failed refund frees its headroom again — that is a real outcome, not an
absence of one.

### 2. A dispute is not a refund

A chargeback is the bank taking money back and telling us afterwards. Recording
it as a refund would make the books say we chose to give it back, and would also
consume refund headroom that no refund used.

- **Lost** posts the loss to `adjustment`.
- **Won** posts nothing. The money never left; a reversal of a reversal invents
  two transactions that did not happen.
- Every dispute raises an `ExceptionItem`, because a chargeback nobody sees is a
  chargeback nobody contests.

`ck_disputes_resolved_at` makes the database refuse a won/lost dispute with no
resolution timestamp.

### 3. Bank funding is reconciled evidence, not a balance edit

Importing a statement line and crediting an account are **separate acts**, and
that separation is the whole point. An import credits nobody. Matching names
both the ledger account and `matched_by`, and
`ck_bank_transfer_receipts_matched` makes a credit without a named account
impossible to store.

`uq_bank_transfer_receipts_line` makes re-importing yesterday's statement
harmless — the failure a manual funding process produces every single time.

An unmatched line stays imported, uncredited, and goes to the exception queue.
Guessing whose transfer it is credits one customer with another's money.

Funding posts at the bank's **value date**, not the reconciliation date.

### 4. Documents freeze their facts

`financial_documents.snapshot` copies amounts, currency, seller identity and tax
reference rather than referencing them.
`test_a_receipt_keeps_the_seller_name_it_was_issued_under` renames the legal
entity afterwards and asserts the document is unchanged — a receipt regenerated
from live data next year would show next year's prices under this year's date.

## Decision gates

**D5 (charging, refund and funding policy) is OPEN.** So:

- `Refund.policy_reference` stores *which* policy was applied as a reference. It
  is **not** a computed entitlement. Nothing in this chunk decides whether a
  given customer is owed a refund; it records the decision and moves the money
  correctly once made.
- Whether a lost dispute claws back the customer's remaining service allowance
  is a policy question and is **not** implemented. `test_a_chargeback_after_the`
  `_service_was_used_is_still_recorded` pins current behaviour: the money is
  recorded, the allowance is untouched.

**D3 (selling legal entity) is OPEN.** `financial_documents` requires a
`seller_legal_entity_id` and stores its snapshot, but no entity is seeded and no
tax treatment is hardcoded. Document numbering is unique per seller and kind, so
adding the real entity later does not renumber anything.

### One pre-existing test is not re-runnable — found, not fixed

`test_package_is_owned_by_and_cascades_with_user` (chunk 03,
`tests/test_activation_model_postgres.py`) fails on the **second** run against
the same database. It creates its tables directly, never cleans up, and asserts
`select(Package).all() == []` globally — so any package row left by any other
test fails it.

The cascade itself is fine. When it failed here, both surviving rows belonged to
live users from other tests, not to the user it had deleted. On a fresh database
it passes.

It is not this chunk's code and I did not touch it: weakening or rewriting
somebody else's cascade test to make my run green is exactly what `AGENTS.md`
forbids. Flagging it for Codex instead. The fix is to scope the assertion to the
user under test, which would also make it fail against the actual defect rather
than against leftover state.

No API routes were added, so `api-spec.md` is unchanged and cannot drift. The
HTTP surface for refunds belongs with the operations screens in chunk 25.

## Not done, deliberately

- **No route handlers.** This is the domain layer and its guarantees.
- **No processor refund calls.** `PaymentProcessorAdapter` has no refund method
  yet; `record_refund_outcome` and `record_refund_unknown` are the seams a real
  adapter drives. Paystack's refund endpoint has **not** been contract-checked
  against current documentation, so no claim is made about it.
- **No tax computation.** D3 gates it.
- **No automatic matching heuristic** for bank lines. A fuzzy match on payer
  reference credits the wrong customer often enough to matter; matching is an
  explicit act with a named actor until someone decides otherwise.
