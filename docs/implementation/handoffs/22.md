## Result

### Previously

The product modelled people as *pilgrims on a tour operator's manifest*. An
organization had one shared email and password, a manifest row was a traveller,
and there was no way to say "these two hundred staff are in the Field team,
billed to CC-2, and none of them administers this account". Chunk 07 fixed
administration by giving memberships to individuals; nobody had yet fixed the
other half, which is everybody an organization buys service **for**.

### Now

An enterprise or government customer can describe their organization — teams,
cost centres, and the people in them — and fill it from the spreadsheet they
already have, seeing exactly what an upload will do before it does it.

**API — new `app/people` package (11 endpoints):** people (list, read, archive,
CSV export), teams, cost centres, and imports (upload/preview, list, rows,
apply, cancel).

**Schema — migration `0041_organization_people`, five new tables:**
`organization_people`, `organization_teams`, `organization_cost_centres`,
`people_imports`, `people_import_rows`. Additive; `organization_members` and the
legacy manifest tables are untouched. Documented as
[data-model §6.57](../../data-model.md) and [api-spec §7.40](../../api-spec.md).

**Dashboard — new `/people` route** with four tabs (people, teams, cost centres,
imports), `lib/people.ts`, and 72 new message keys in each locale.

**Refactor within scope:** the CSV formula-injection guard moved from
`app/reports/service.py` to `app/csv_export.py` and is now shared. The second
export is the one somebody writes without remembering the rule, and this chunk
adds a second export built almost entirely from customer-typed text.

**The decisions a reviewer should look at:**

1. **People and members are different tables, and that is the enforcement.**
   The assignment's rule is *importing an email must not grant organization
   privilege*. There is no code path from `app/people` to
   `organization_members`, and the import format has no column that could feed
   one. Two thousand imported staff produce two thousand recipients and zero
   administrators.
2. **`Role` is deliberately not a recognised column.** It was initially matched
   as a synonym for a job title; that was removed, because the word is
   overloaded in exactly the dangerous direction — a customer writing
   `Role,owner` means a job title to themselves and reads as an access grant to
   anybody skimming the file. `Job Title`, `Title` and `Position` all still work.
3. **`user_id` on a person stays null until they claim the record.** An
   imported email is a claim *about* somebody; binding it to an existing account
   would let an importer attach a colleague's identity by typing their address.
4. **Duplicate identity is three partial unique indexes, per organization.**
   Per organization because two customers may employ the same contractor;
   partial because most of those fields are legitimately empty. This is what
   makes the same file uploaded twice an update rather than a second Ada Obi,
   whichever code path does the writing.
5. **Preview and apply are separate acts, and the preview is computed by the
   code that will do the work.** An administrator approving "412 new, 6 errors"
   is approving a fact rather than an estimate — which is only true because the
   parser is pure and shared between the two paths.
6. **An apply is resumable by construction.** Each row records its own outcome
   as it is applied, so a process that dies leaves the import in `applying` with
   its rows saying which people already exist. There is nothing to remember and
   nothing to guess.
7. **A row reports every reason it is wrong.** Fixing a two-thousand-line file
   one error per upload is how people give up around the fourth round trip.
8. **Structure is never invented.** A row naming a team or cost centre the
   organization does not have is an *error*, not a silent creation: an import
   that creates a department from a typo builds a structure nobody chose and a
   cost centre nobody is watching.
9. **The path's `organization_id` is discarded.** Every handler reads the
   tenant from the resolved context, including the CSV export.

## Migrations and compatibility

- **Integration renumbering.** The submitted `0038` migration and §6.54/§7.37
  amendments collided with accepted V02, V03, chunk 20 and chunk 21 work. The
  integrated branch uses `0041_organization_people`, §6.57 and §7.40.
- **Fresh install:** five tables and three enums. Every endpoint answers; a new
  organization sees empty lists.
- **Old-data upgrade:** additive only, and nothing is backfilled. Existing
  manifest rows are **not** converted into people: the two models mean different
  things, and inventing a person from a pilgrim row would put a guess into a
  customer's staff list. Migrating an operator who wants their manifest as
  people is a data exercise with a human in it.
- **Old-client compatibility:** every endpoint is new; no existing response
  shape changes. The dashboard gains a route and changes none.
- **Rollback:** `alembic downgrade 0040_account_and_support` drops exactly what
  the upgrade created. Development only — it discards an organization's people
  and their import history.
- **Configuration:** none added. No new secret names.
- **Dependencies:** none added, API or dashboard.

## UI or integration evidence

- **Dashboard `/people`**, four tabs, English and French, verified by the vitest
  suite rather than by screenshot. The assertions worth naming: the page states
  in words that adding somebody does not grant them access; an import preview
  shows counts and per-row reasons before anything is applied; a file that could
  not be read says why; and the apply button is disabled when a file has nothing
  usable in it.
- **A copy change forced by a safety guard.** `featureRetirement.test.ts` scans
  the catalogs for retired welfare vocabulary, and the word *Checking* contains
  `checkin`. The copy and the message key were renamed rather than the guard
  relaxed — `AGENTS.md` is explicit that a check is never weakened to make
  something pass, and a blunt guard producing an occasional false positive is
  working as intended.
- **Sensitive fields:** no secret reaches a log, a document or a fixture. The
  raw import payload is retained in `people_import_rows` so a preview can be
  re-rendered; it is the customer's own data and it is never rendered into an
  export without `csv_safe`.

## Known gaps and handoff

- **The legacy manifest surface is untouched.** `app/manifests`, its routes and
  the dashboard's `/manifests` pages still exist and still speak of pilgrims.
  This chunk generalizes the model; retiring the old one is the removal sequence
  in `IMPLEMENTATION-PLAN.md` §7 — inventory, stop new enrollment, handle queued
  work and old clients, backfill, verify, then drop — and that sequence needs a
  compatibility window and a decision about existing operator data. **Doing it
  quietly inside this chunk would have deleted a customer's history to tidy a
  screen.**
- **A person cannot be edited through the API.** They are created and updated by
  import, and archived by hand. A single-person edit endpoint is an obvious next
  addition and was left out rather than guessed at: whether an administrator may
  change somebody's email after an import is a question about identity, not
  about CRUD.
- **`organization_id` reaches the dashboard through `localStorage`.** That is
  how the existing pages do it, and it is the wrong long-term shape — the
  organization a session is acting for belongs in the session. Flagged here
  rather than changed, because it is an auth-path decision that would touch
  every existing page.
- **Teams do not nest.** A department inside a division is a real enterprise
  shape and a `parent_id` was deliberately not added: nesting changes every
  query that scopes by team, and no acceptance criterion asks for it yet.
- **For Codex:** the thing most worth attacking is `_upsert_person`'s matching
  order — reference, then email, then phone. A customer who reuses an employee
  number after somebody leaves will merge two people, and whether that is
  correct depends on a policy nobody has stated. It is the one place this chunk
  decides something about identity on the customer's behalf.
- **No merge, deployment, store submission or production deletion was
  performed**, and none is authorized by this assignment.
