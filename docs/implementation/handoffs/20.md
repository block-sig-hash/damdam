## Result

### Previously

Chunks 15–17 built the connectivity lifecycle, usage reconciliation and spending
controls — including a full AES-256-GCM credential vault with one-time,
person-bound delivery grants — as services with **no HTTP surface and no
application wiring at all**. Nothing in `main.py` constructed a
`ConnectivityService`, a `UsageService`, a `ControlService` or a
`CredentialVault`. The My Line tab was a placeholder naming this chunk.

### Now

A holder can see their line, understand what is and is not working, read what
calls cost, install their eSIM under protection, and be told the truth when any
of that is not possible.

**API — new `app/line` package (5 endpoints):**

- `GET /v1/me/lines`, `GET /v1/me/lines/{entitlement_id}` — the holder's view.
- `POST …/installation/grant` → a single-use, 10-minute, person-bound
  authorization. **Returns no profile.**
- `POST …/installation/redeem` → the profile, once.
- `POST …/installation/confirm` → the device reporting what it did, in both
  directions, returning the refreshed line.

Plus the wiring chunks 15–17 never had, and two new settings:
`ACTIVATION_MATERIAL_KEY` (base64 of exactly 32 bytes, validated at startup,
empty = no delivery) and `INTERNET_DIALER_ENABLED` (false, and raises at startup
if set, because V04/V05 are not accepted).

**Mobile — `src/screens/MyLine/`**, `src/api/lineClient.ts`,
`src/services/screenPrivacy.ts` and an Android `ScreenPrivacyModule`
(`FLAG_SECURE`).

**The decisions a reviewer should look at:**

1. **A read never returns a profile.** `GET /v1/me/lines/{id}` is polled by a
   status screen, a pull-to-refresh and a push handler. Delivery is two calls
   because the first half is retryable and the second is not: a customer who
   taps "show my eSIM" twice mints two grants and spends one, rather than
   spending a one-time profile on the first tap. Both delivery responses carry
   `Cache-Control: no-store`.
2. **404 for another account's line, never 403.** "Does not exist" and "is not
   yours" must be indistinguishable. Chunk 19's review found this exact class of
   defect in the checkout routes; the same mistake here would expose activation
   material, which cannot be rotated after it leaks.
3. **Holder, not payer.** Every route resolves `entitlements.holder_user_id`. An
   organization buying for its staff is the payer; the holder is whose phone the
   profile goes on.
4. **Nothing is inferred from a neighbouring state.** Network attachment is not
   derived from activation, voice capability is not derived from the plan having
   voice in it, and "installed" is only ever what a device reported. Four
   separate pills, each with when it was observed.
5. **`reinstall_available` is always false, and says why.** Telnyx documents that
   a downloaded profile cannot be re-downloaded; a replacement device needs a new
   purchase. A reinstall button would offer something the supplier cannot do.
6. **Screen protection is engaged before the code is fetched, not after**, and
   where the platform has nothing — iOS cannot block a screenshot at all — the
   screen says so rather than rendering the code as if protected. The flag is
   released on the way out and on unmount, because `FLAG_SECURE` lives on the
   Activity window and one left behind makes every later screen uncapturable.

**Deliberately deferred / out of scope:** no QR *image* is rendered. Chunk 15's
vault produces an LPA string, not a supplier QR URL, and generating one would
mean a new dependency; the screen gives the two fields a phone actually asks for
(SM-DP+ address and activation code, split from the LPA) plus the platform
install path. No top-up purchase flow — chunk 17 built the service and the
purchase of one is not in this assignment; My Line reports applied and pending
top-ups. No internet dialer (V04).

**Refactors within scope:**

- `src/screens/Purchase/format.ts` moved to `src/utils/format.ts`. Two chunks now
  format the same money and byte values, and a second copy of `money()` is how
  one screen comes to display an amount differently from another.
- `number_status` is derived from the product's **`NumberPolicy`**, not from the
  carrier line's `voice_enabled` flag. A voice-capable line sold on a data-only
  plan is still a plan with no number, and the proxy would have reported it as
  waiting for one forever.
- `ready_to_use` and `usage.expired` both read the **top-up-extended** expiry.
  Reading `entitlements.expires_at` directly would report a line as dead while
  its owner had already paid to keep it — and the two fields would have
  contradicted each other inside one response.

**A defect found and fixed during this chunk, worth naming:** the redeem route
originally guarded a cross-line token by checking `delivery_count == 0` on the
addressed line. The vault authorizes by *holder*, so a customer holding two
lines could present line A's token against line B — and where line B had already
been delivered once, the fixed-zero check passed and handed back line A's
profile under line B's id, burning a one-time eSIM nobody asked about. The route
now compares the count read **before** the redemption against the one after, and
raises before the commit so the vault's write to line A rolls back.
`test_a_token_for_one_line_cannot_be_spent_against_another` was verified to fail
against the original check and pass against the fix.

**Contract change to an accepted chunk:** `GET /v1/me/services` gains a nullable
`entitlement_id` (§7.37 amends §7.35), so Home's "install now" opens *that* line
instead of a list. Null until provisioning grants an entitlement.

## Migrations and compatibility

- **No migration.** No table, column or constraint is added. Every model read
  was created by chunks 05, 09–15 and 17. `alembic upgrade head` is unchanged.
- **Fresh install:** every endpoint answers. With no activation key configured,
  `GET /v1/me/lines/{id}` reports
  `credential_unavailable_reason: "material_key_unavailable"` and the delivery
  endpoints answer **503** — fail closed, and it says which way.
- **Old-data upgrade:** nothing read that did not already exist. Entitlements,
  installations, carrier lines, credentials and grants are all chunk 15's.
- **Old-client compatibility:** `GET /v1/me/services` gains one **nullable**
  field; a client ignoring it behaves exactly as before. Everything else is new
  routes. `app/i18n/catalog.py` gains 5 keys per locale and changes none.
- **Rollback:** revert the branch. Five endpoints disappear, the My Line tab
  reverts to a placeholder, and the `FLAG_SECURE` module goes with it. No
  irreversible step. Sealed credentials written before a rollback remain sealed
  and readable afterwards — the key reference is stored on the row.
- **Configuration:** two new settings, **names only**:
  `ACTIVATION_MATERIAL_KEY` (base64 of 32 bytes; **empty in every environment
  today**, and empty means no profile can be delivered) and
  `INTERNET_DIALER_ENABLED` (false; raises at startup if set). No key value
  appears in this repository, this handoff, or any fixture — the tests generate
  one per run from `os.urandom`.
- **Dependencies:** **none added**, API or mobile. Nothing new reaches the
  `aarch64` instance.

## Known gaps and handoff

1. **D1 is open, so no profile exists to install.** No Telnyx account, no
   provisioned line, no activation material. The whole delivery path is
   exercised against locally sealed credentials. *Missing input: a Telnyx
   account and a provisioned test line. Owner: the founder.*
2. **`ACTIVATION_MATERIAL_KEY` is unset everywhere.** Until an operator supplies
   one, every deployment reports `material_key_unavailable` and refuses to
   deliver. That is the designed failure, not an outage — but it does mean the
   delivery path has never run in any deployed environment.
3. **No QR image.** The vault stores an LPA string; chunk 15 does not produce a
   supplier QR URL, and rendering one client-side needs a dependency this chunk
   did not add. The screen gives the split SM-DP+ address and activation code —
   which is what a phone's manual entry actually asks for — plus Android's direct
   install. **If a reviewer wants a QR, it is a dependency decision, not an
   oversight.**
4. **`ScreenPrivacyModule.kt` has never been compiled.** No Android SDK here. It
   follows the existing modules in `EsimDownloadPackage` closely, but CI's build
   is the first real check, and `protectScreen()` returning `failed` on a broken
   build is the designed behaviour — the warning stays on screen.
5. **iOS cannot block screenshots and this does not try.** There is no public
   API. The screen warns instead. An `applicationWillResignActive` blur for the
   app-switcher snapshot would be a genuine additional mitigation and is **not**
   implemented — it is a separate iOS-side change and would be worth its own
   scope.
6. **Top-ups are reported, not sold.** Chunk 17 built the service; buying one is
   not in this assignment. My Line shows applied and pending top-ups and says
   when a paid one is not yet usable.
7. **For Codex:** the two places worth the most scrutiny are
   `CredentialVault.redeem`'s `FOR UPDATE` path as it is now reached over HTTP
   (the concurrency test covers the service; the route adds a
   `credential_for` read before it, and the ordering is worth confirming), and
   `MyLineFlow`'s `enterInstall` → `reveal` sequence, where the protection call
   must precede the network call in every path including the retry.
8. **No merge, deployment, store submission, vendor message, live purchase or
   production data deletion was performed.**
